<objective>
Turn the measurement into a decision, honestly, and close the phase's records.

Purpose: Plan 03-06 produced numbers. Numbers are not a decision, and the gap between them is where
this kind of work usually goes wrong — a margin gets read as a win because a win was hoped for, a
limitation gets softened because it is inconvenient, and a result from a broken data source gets
reported beside a sound one with no distinction drawn. This phase has already been bitten twice by
exactly that class of error: a lead-time boundary characterized from one of four model runs, and a
raw-forecast arm scoring a perfect zero because the forecast and the truth were the same numbers.
Both were caught because someone looked at a too-good result and investigated instead of accepting
it. The write-up has to hold the same standard.

Output: the completed nine-section decision record; an updated ROADMAP Phase 3 entry reflecting what
was actually executed; a corrected and completed `03-VALIDATION.md`; and one consolidated
AI-use disclosure entry.

**The verdict is read against the Expectation as committed (Success Criterion 5).** Open the
Expectation section, read what it predicted, then read the measured results, and write the Verdict
as a comparison between the two. Where the measurement contradicts the Expectation, say so
explicitly — that contradiction is a finding, often a more informative one than a confirmation, and
it is the entire reason the Expectation was written before the numbers existed. Do not edit the
Expectation.

**Both results, clearly separated (D-04's disposition).** The primary result is the 24-hour
realizable-lead comparison. The legacy 1-hour arm is real, built, tested and committed in Wave 1,
and D-04 explicitly keeps it as a documented non-primary curiosity rather than deleting working
code. It must appear in the record, in its own clearly-labelled section, with the bit-identity
finding attached so prominently that no reader can quote its raw-forecast score without the caveat
travelling with it. It must not appear in the primary verdict's candidate comparison.

**What this phase does not do.** It does not change any runtime behavior, and it does not wire a
winner into the shipped forecast methods. That is a separate future phase contingent on this one's
result and requiring its own decision about fetch-fallback behavior. The record should say what it
recommends and name that boundary, so a future reader knows the recommendation was deliberate
rather than abandoned.
</objective>
