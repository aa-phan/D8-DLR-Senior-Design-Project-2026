<objective>
Build the two machine-learning correction families D-02's arms 3 and 4 need, as a standalone module with its
own tests, and resolve both of the parameterization questions CONTEXT.md left open — on measured validation
evidence, not on a lean.

Purpose: these are the only genuinely new modelling components in the phase, and they carry the two decisions
most likely to be made badly. Arm 3 can be pooled three different ways and arm 4 can be targeted two different
ways; picking either by intuition would undercut the whole point of an evidence-based comparison. Keeping them
in their own module, fit and selected against a validation split before the harness ever calls them, means the
selection is testable in isolation and the leakage risk is contained to one file with one rule: never touch
the test slice.

Separating this module from `forecast_experiment.py` also lets it be built in parallel with Plan 03-03, and
mirrors how `forecast_training.py` keeps its estimator factories above and separate from its harness.

Output: `backend/forecast_corrections.py`, its test file, and a small config addition.

**Resolving CONTEXT's arm 3 pooling question.** Three modes are implemented and all three are scored on the
validation set: one model per endpoint city; one model per corridor pooled over its two endpoint cities with
an endpoint indicator feature; and one model pooled over all four cities across both corridors. The third is
defensible and worth measuring because the two corridors share an identical `ConductorConfig` except for line
azimuth, and pooling across them quadruples the training rows. Which one wins is a measurement, recorded with
all three numbers, not a choice made here.

**Resolving CONTEXT's arm 4 target question.** Two parameterizations are implemented and both are scored on the
validation set: predicting the observed convective heat-loss level directly, and predicting the scale-free
logarithm of the ratio between the observed and forecast convective terms. RESEARCH.md's Assumption A6 flags
that the convective term is the maximum of a forced and a natural expression and is therefore non-smooth, so a
regression on its level may behave badly near that kink; the log-ratio is scale-free and better-behaved across
it. That is a reason to measure both, not a reason to pre-pick the log-ratio. The fraction of validation rows
sitting near the regime boundary is recorded alongside, so a future reader can see whether the kink was
actually in play.
</objective>
