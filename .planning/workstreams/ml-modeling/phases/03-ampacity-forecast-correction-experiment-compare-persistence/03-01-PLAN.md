<objective>
Land one real, production-quality end-to-end slice of this experiment: a genuine NWP forecast archive is
fetched from Open-Meteo's Historical Forecast API, its effective lead time is *measured* against the
Single-Runs API rather than trusted, and — on the subset where that lead is provably exactly one hour — a raw
forecast pushed through IEEE 738 is scored head-to-head against naive persistence on identical held-out test
timestamps, for one city, with the idealized-latency caveat printed alongside the number.

Purpose: this phase's two genuine unknowns are (a) whether the bulk forecast product's lead time is what the
research measured it to be, and (b) whether a paired forecast/truth frame can be assembled without the
persistence baseline silently degrading into a 6-hour-stale predictor. Both are architectural dead ends if
they fail, and both fail cheaply here — one city, two arms — rather than after five more plans have been built
on top of them. Everything downstream (the other five arms, the second corridor, the ML corrections) is a
re-composition of machinery that already exists in `forecast_training.py` and `anomaly_training.py`.

Output: the forecast provider adapter, the lead-time calibration gate, the three-way calendar splitter, the
first slice of the experiment harness, a new `FORECAST_EXPERIMENT_*` config block, and four new test files.

**Anchored pair frame — the contract this plan establishes and every later plan inherits.** One row per scored
valid time `T` where `T.hour % 6 == 1`, carrying three time-distinct column groups, all assembled from the
FULL unfiltered hourly series *before* the filter is applied:

| Group | Time | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `anchor_*` | **T - 1 h** | Archive API truth (the last observation a forecaster would actually have had) |
| `truth_*` | **T** | Archive API truth (what actually happened) |
| `fcst_*` | **T**, issued at T - 1 h | Historical Forecast API, `models=ecmwf_ifs` |

The `hour % 6 == 1` filter selects **which rows are scored**. It is never the source of a lagged value. This
is the single most important invariant in the phase: filtered rows are six hours apart, so a persistence
baseline computed from them would be a six-hour-stale predictor presented as a one-hour one, every forecast
arm would beat it, and the phase would publish a falsely positive result — the same category of honesty
failure, one level up, that this phase was created to correct.

**Lead-time contingency — resolved here, not left open.** CONTEXT.md scoped three fuzzy contingencies (a)/(b)/(c)
on the assumption that the bulk product's lead time was unknown. It is not. RESEARCH.md live-verified that with
`models=ecmwf_ifs` pinned on both endpoints, the bulk value at valid time `T` is bit-identical to the
`run = T.floor('6h')` single run's value at lead `T.hour % 6`, across three cities and three seasons, with a
clean cut at lead 6. So `lead_hours = valid_time.hour % 6` is a deterministic rule to be VERIFIED, not a
distribution to be estimated, and CONTEXT's option (c) is promoted from a probabilistic filter to an exact one.
The gate is built as an exact-match verifier, and it is expected — correctly — to fail on the unfiltered series.

**Scope resolution: this tracer scores at CITY level, not corridor level.** The weakest-link reduction over a
corridor's two endpoint cities is deferred to Plan 03-03, which consumes the `corridor_weakest_link` helper
Plan 03-02 extracts. The alternative — an inline `np.minimum` here that Plan 03-03 would then delete — is
exactly the duplicate-then-replace pattern RESEARCH.md's "Don't Hand-Roll" table forbids, and two independent
copies of a non-obvious tie-break rule will drift. City-level ampacity is a real, meaningful score in its own
right and is the granularity the forecast actually arrives at.
</objective>
