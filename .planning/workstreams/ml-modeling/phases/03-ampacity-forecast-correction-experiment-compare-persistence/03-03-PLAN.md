<objective>
Prove the entire 24-hour realizable-lead architecture end-to-end on one thin path before any arm is
built on top of it: fetch a genuine 24-hour-lead NWP forecast from Open-Meteo's Previous Runs API
under its own pinned model, **measure** (never assume) that the product's effective lead is
`24 + (hour % 6)` hours rather than the flat 24 hours both the vendor docs and D-04's own text
claim, generalize Wave 1's anchored-pair-frame contract by a `lead_hours` parameter instead of
cloning it, and score 24-hour persistence against a raw 24-hour forecast through IEEE 738 on the
**validation** split for one city.

Purpose: D-04 pivoted this phase's primary comparison to the 24-hour lead because the 1-hour design
hit a bit-identical forecast/truth data artifact. That pivot has exactly two architectural unknowns
worth failing cheaply on, and both fail here — one city, two arms — rather than after four more
plans are stacked on them:

1. **Does the forecast genuinely differ from truth at 24 hours?** If arm 2 scores another exact
   0.000 MAE, the pivot has not solved anything and the phase needs a third data strategy.
   (RESEARCH refuted this with ~140 compared hours across 2 cities, 6 dates and 2 variables — zero
   exact matches — but that refutation was measured outside this repo's code, so the tracer is
   where it becomes a fact about *this pipeline*.)
2. **Is the lead actually exact?** RESEARCH's single most important finding this refresh is that
   `previous_day1` is stitched from six-hourly runs exactly like the legacy bulk product, just
   offset by +24 h — so the `hour % 6` filter discipline is NOT eliminated by the pivot, only
   re-pointed from remainder 1 to remainder 0. D-04's own text says "no calibration gate needed at
   all." That sentence is wrong, and building on it is precisely the mistake ("never trust the
   product's stated lead time") this phase was created to stop making.

Output: `fetch_previous_runs` and its own `PREVIOUS_RUNS_MODEL_ID` pin; the 24-hour offset-lead
verification grafted onto the existing calibration module rather than re-derived in a new one; the
`lead_hours`-generalized anchored-pair contract with a lead-aware cache key; the new
`FORECAST_EXPERIMENT_*` 24-hour constants; and one live validation-split score for AUSTIN.

**Why the model pin is a NEW constant, not a reuse (D-04, RESEARCH Pattern 1).** The legacy
`FORECAST_MODEL_ID` on the Previous-Runs endpoint returns HTTP 200 with an all-null array for
essentially the whole candidate window — re-confirmed live during this planning session:
a 2024-06-01 AUSTIN request came back 24/24 null. Its Previous-Runs archive is a *different,
independently provisioned* archive from the Historical-Forecast/Single-Runs archive the legacy arm
depends on, and depth on one says nothing about depth on another. Two different pins across the two
endpoints is the CORRECT state, not an inconsistency for a later refactor to tidy away
(RESEARCH Pitfall 2) — the new function's docstring must say so in those terms.

**Why `lead_hours` is generalized rather than cloned (RESEARCH Pattern 3).** Wave 1's
`anchored_pair_frame`/`assert_exact_lead`/`filter_to_exact_lead` already implement the right
contract — anchor/truth/fcst column groups, the anchor built from the FULL unfiltered series before
any filter, an automated gate that the anchor gap equals the claimed lead. They only hardcode
`pd.Timedelta(hours=1)` and the legacy remainder. A parallel 24-hour copy is the exact
"Don't Hand-Roll" failure the phase's own research warns about, and the two copies would drift.

**The test split is off-limits in this plan.** The tracer scores the VALIDATION split. Plan 03-06
commits the Expectation record and only then touches held-out test rows, per Success Criterion 5's
no-peeking discipline. Scoring test rows here, "just to see," would spend that discipline before the
phase has anything to spend it on.
</objective>
